MAMA-SAUTI — ASR Benchmark Report

Intron Sahara CodeSwitch Africa Challenge · Health Track  |  Generated 2026-09-13  |  github.com/bellahellam/Intron_Sahara_Codeswitch_Africa_Challenge

1. Purpose

MAMA-SAUTI is a postpartum mental health screening assistant that listens to a Kenyan mother speak in natural Kiswahili-English code-switching and produces a PHQ-9/GAD-7 screening record. The benchmark asks one question: does ASR quality on code-switched Swahili change the clinical decision this product makes?

The dominant failure on this type of speech is switch-boundary deletion — the model returns fluent monolingual Swahili with the embedded English gone. In this setting that English carries the affective vocabulary ("stress", "depressed", "I can't cope"). Aggregate WER cannot distinguish losing a filler from losing the only clinical content in a sentence. This benchmark adds two metrics WER cannot see: EESR (Embedded-English Span Recall) and EESR-clinical (restricted to spans containing an affective term).

4. Metrics

Metric↓/↑Rationale
WER↓Standard. Reported under Intron's normalisation for comparability with published baselines.
CER↓More robust than WER for morphologically rich languages where a token error affects multiple characters.
EESR↑For each gold [[EN]]-tagged span, did its token sequence survive? Directly measures switch-boundary deletion. Net-new metric absent from Intron's benchmarking repo.
EESR-clinical↑EESR restricted to spans containing an affective term. A model can have healthy EESR while this collapses — and this is the subset that determines whether screening works.
CMI-Δ↓Difference in Code-Mixing Index between reference and hypothesis. Measures whether the model flattens the utterance's switching structure.
p95 latency/chunk↓Deployment constraint: product targets a mid-range Android in a low-connectivity clinic.

CIR and SPR are computed but shown as raw counts only (n=1 in this corpus — a rate over n=1 is not a rate). Tier 3 (band-flip, |ΔPHQ-9|) is absent by design: 11/12 conversations are non-psychiatric with PHQ-9 totals near zero; no transcription error can move the band. Stated explicitly, not hidden.

6. Qualitative Findings

ElevenLabs Scribe v2

Strengths: Best WER and CER. Strong English span retention (EESR 89.1%). Low latency. Handles code-switching without language forcing.

Weaknesses: EESR-clinical (80%) lower than Whisper auto despite better WER — some affective terms transcribed in paraphrase. Cloud-only; data leaves device (consent implications). Cost at scale.

Sahara v2.5 (shipped, corrections OFF)

Strengths: Lowest latency (p95 7.4 s/chunk). Competitive WER for an African-language model. No switch-boundary catastrophe.

Weaknesses: EESR-clinical 73.3% — lowest among models with a score. Corrections deliberately disabled; corrections-ON would score differently but introduces an undisclosed post-processor that makes the safety scan non-deterministic.

Whisper v3 (auto-detect)

Strengths: Best EESR-clinical (93.3%) — preserves affective English better than any other model. Best CMI-Δ (3.37). No API cost.

Weaknesses: Higher WER (0.332). p95 latency 43 s/chunk on CPU — not viable for real-time use without GPU.

Whisper v3 (sw forced)

Strengths: Lower aggregate WER than auto-detect (0.289). Faster than auto-detect.

Weaknesses: The critical finding of this benchmark. Forcing language="sw" causes the model to delete all embedded English. EESR-clinical is unmeasurable — zero affective English spans survived across all 12 conversations. CMI-Δ 10.32 confirms the switching structure is flattened. A lower WER while losing all clinical content is the exact failure mode this benchmark was built to detect. This configuration is unsafe for this use case.

Jacaranda-Health/ASR-STT

Strengths: Regional incumbent with direct maternal-health deployment experience. No API cost. Relevant baseline: if Sahara did not beat this model, that would be a publishable negative finding.

Weaknesses: Worst on every metric — WER 0.565, EESR 35.5%, EESR-clinical 20.0%. Loses the majority of embedded English and scores near-zero on affective terms for most conversations.

7. Key Finding

The question is not WER — it is what survives at the switch boundary.
Whisper sw-forced achieves lower WER than auto-detect but loses all clinical English. Jacaranda, the regional incumbent, loses most of it. Sahara achieves competitive WER with the lowest latency but lower EESR-clinical than either Whisper condition. ElevenLabs leads on standard metrics. For a product where the clinical decision depends on affective English vocabulary surviving transcription, EESR-clinical is the metric that matters.

Sahara v2.5 (corrections OFF) is the deployed configuration because it is the only model that combines: (a) sub-10 s chunk latency for real-time use in a low-connectivity clinic, (b) no switch-boundary deletion catastrophe, and (c) a deterministic transcript that can be quoted verbatim back to the patient.
